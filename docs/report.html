Part 1 · Data status

What the record holds, and what it lost

Every dataset is a row per hour. Line status is the longest series. Lift and station-notice history starts 8 to 9 days later than line status, because an old "prune at 700 documents" rule deleted the oldest hours.

Finding 1 · Reliability

Victoria and Central are delayed almost half the time

46%

Of the hours the Victoria line was running, 46% carried a minor or severe delay. Central follows at 40.5%, then the Metropolitan at 30.5%. On 27 Sep, 23 of 24 Victoria readings showed a delay. District and Piccadilly look better but lose a quarter of their hours to part closures.

Finding 2 · Timing

Delays build all day and peak at 18:00, weekends included

27%

At 18:00 London time, 27% of running Tube lines carried a delay. The share climbs from 11% at 06:00 and stays above 22% from 20:00 until midnight. Weekday and weekend are identical: 19.1% each.

Finding 3 · Step-free access

Six lifts have been out the entire time we have watched

19out now

19 lifts at 17 stations are out of service right now, and eight have been out for more than a week. Six have not been back once since per-lift tracking began on 30 Sep. Station messages put the end dates at "late October" for Elephant & Castle, "Autumn 2026" for Wembley Park and "Spring 2027" for Canada Water.

Finding 4 · Crowding

The real rush hour is 18:00, not 09:00

22% busier

Across the 36 tracked stations, the weekday evening peak is 22% busier than the morning peak (31% against 26% of baseline). Fifteen stations peak at 18:00 and seven at 17:00, while only seven peak at 09:00. Weekends flatten out at about 20% from 13:00 to 19:00.

Finding 5 · Frequency

The core sees a train within half a minute. The edge waits 11.

32× gap

Part 2 · What changes

From a chat session to a collector that cannot lose hours

  1. Collection moves to GitHub Actions. It runs every hour on the hour, polls everything (no night-time skipping) and costs zero tokens.
  2. History lives in git. All 3,271 readings fit in about 0.8 MB packed; a full year adds roughly 10 MB.
  3. The dashboard is a static page built from that data on every run. Opening it costs nothing.
  4. Nothing is ever pruned. A daily check raises an alert if any hour is missing.

Method. Generated 24 Oct 2026, 16:18 UTC from the full export of the Interchange Navigator database (3,271 TfL readings, 3,386 documents in all). TfL data is published under the Open Government Licence. "Delayed" means a status of Minor Delays or Severe Delays; hours where a line shows only "Service Closed" are excluded from line percentages.
